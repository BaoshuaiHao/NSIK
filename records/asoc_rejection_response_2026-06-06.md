## Reviewer Comments

The primary issue of this paper is that the experimental evaluation is not sufficiently convincing to support the claimed contributions.

Although NSIK is presented as a competitive method, it fails to outperform the strongest baseline, GLAR, on most benchmarks, with lower average Hits@10 on WN18RR-ind (92.86 vs. 93.55) and FB15k237-ind (94.64 vs. 95.06), as well as lower average AUC-PR across all three datasets.

The baseline selection is incomplete, as several recent state-of-the-art inductive KGC approaches are not considered, making it difficult to assess the true competitiveness of the method.

The implementation details are insufficiently justified, with key hyperparameters such as the number of structural roles, diffusion steps, Top-100 source selection, and the 1:1 sampling ratio appearing heuristic and lacking theoretical or empirical motivation.

The ablation study reports large performance drops after removing individual modules, but no statistical significance analysis is provided, leaving uncertainty regarding the robustness of these conclusions.

The parameter sensitivity analysis is limited to a single parameter and explores only a narrow range of values, while the influence of other important hyperparameters remains unexplored.

Furthermore, the noise robustness experiment relies solely on synthetic noise generated by random tail replacement, which may not realistically reflect noise patterns in real-world knowledge graphs.

Overall, the experimental results demonstrate only marginal improvements and do not provide sufficiently strong evidence that the proposed structural prior framework offers substantial advantages over existing methods.

## Action Items To Consider

- Add recent inductive KGC baselines beyond GLAR and report direct comparisons.
- Strengthen experiments where NSIK currently underperforms GLAR, especially average Hits@10 and AUC-PR.
- Add statistical significance tests for main and ablation results.
- Expand sensitivity analysis to structural roles, diffusion steps, Top-K source selection, and sampling ratio.
- Justify key hyperparameters with theory, validation curves, or controlled experiments.
- Add more realistic noisy-KG experiments beyond random tail replacement.
